## Description (4000 max)

App Store descriptions are plain text — no bold, no markdown. Paste as-is.

Time Rolls is a photo trivia game. Each day brings a Daily Roll — a handful of
rounds, four photographs at a time, and one question to answer.

Pick what to play:
- Geography — which map shows Japan? Which country borders France?
- Cars — which car is a Ford Mustang? Which one is the 1965?
- Film Stars — who starred in Casablanca? Who won their first Oscar for Roman Holiday?
- Sports Stars — who played for the Green Bay Packers? Who was named Super Bowl MVP?
- Famous Faces, Famous Artworks, Animals, Plants and Travel Landmarks
- Your own photos — which photo is from Italy? Which came first?

Every answer comes with a "Did you know?" card, so each round leaves you knowing a
little more than before.

Easy to play
- Tap a photo, or say "one", "two", "three" or "four" out loud.
- Swipe right, or say "next", to move on. Nothing is timed.
- A wrong answer gets another try, never a buzzer.
- Large type, high contrast, and a voice that can read the question aloud.
- Open any photo full screen and share it or save it.

Your photos stay yours
Your photos never leave your device. They are shown on screen and, for the Things
game, looked at by your device's own photo recognition. There is no sign-in, no
account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking of any kind. Nothing is sold.
Shared photos go through the iOS share sheet, never through us, and our own
photographs carry their photographer's credit with them.

Free
Time Rolls is free. There is nothing to buy and no paid tier.

Made by Mission Peak.

## Review notes

No account or sign-in — tap through onboarding and play. On first launch the app
asks for photo library access; declining is fine, there is a "Play with the
built-in photos instead" path that uses the curated packs alone. Microphone and
speech access are optional and only used while a round waits for an answer;
recognition is on-device. Pack photographs are fetched from our storage provider
(OneBucket) over signed, short-lived URLs; the request carries nothing about the
user.
