# Time Rolls Privacy Statement

This is the privacy statement for Time Rolls, the photo game built by Mission Peak, LLC for older adults and the people who care for them. It explains what the app collects, why, how long it's kept, and the choices and rights you have over your data.

Time Rolls is built by Mission Peak, LLC, 10311 Regency Station Dr, Fairfax Station, VA 22039, USA. Mission Peak, LLC is the data controller for this statement. You can reach us at [information@mission-peak.com](mailto:information@mission-peak.com).

## 2. Our approach

Time Rolls shows people their own photographs and asks gentle questions about them. It has no user accounts, no login, and no email address. We are built to know as little about you as possible.

Your photographs are the most personal thing this app touches, and they never leave your device. They are displayed on your screen and examined by your own device's photo recognition. No image is ever uploaded, and no description of what is in one is ever sent anywhere.

One thing does leave the device to help us improve the game: anonymous counts of how it is played (see “Usage statistics” below). You can turn that off at any time.

## 3. What we collect and why

**Nothing that identifies you.** There is no account, no sign-in, no name, no email address, no phone number and no advertising identifier. There is no advertising code in the app, and it never reads your device's advertising identifier.

**Stays on your device.** Your photographs and everything derived from them; which photographs a caregiver has asked never to be shown, kept as identifiers only; your settings; your stars, streak and how many sessions you have played; a record of which questions have been asked recently, so they are not repeated; and downloaded copies of the photographs in our photo packs.

**Photographs.** The app asks for photo library access so it can show you your own pictures. Your device's own photo recognition looks at them to spot everyday things — a dog, a cake, a beach — to tell a photograph of a place from a close-up of a face, and to leave out screenshots, receipts and blurry shots. On devices with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model may also describe a photograph to itself so the app can leave out ones that are not memories. All of this happens entirely on the device. No photograph, label or description is sent anywhere.

**Microphone.** If you turn on answering out loud, speech is recognised on the device itself, with on-device recognition explicitly required. Nothing is recorded, stored or sent. The microphone is open only while a round is waiting for an answer, and not at all when sound is switched off.

**Photo locations.** For place questions, the coordinates recorded in a photograph are used to work out the name of the place, using Apple's location lookup. One set of coordinates is looked up for each area about 11 km across, and the answer is kept on your device. This is the only thing about a photograph that leaves your device — never the image, and never a description of it.

**Where you are, only if you ask.** “Places near you” is off unless you turn it on. When it is on, a rough location — rounded to about a kilometre — is sent to Wikipedia to ask what notable places are nearby. It is not stored, nothing identifies your device, and no photograph is involved.

**Our photo packs.** The photographs in the packs are fetched from OneBucket, our storage provider, as you play. Each request names a photograph and says nothing about you — no identifier, no account, nothing about your own pictures. Each is cached on your device so it is fetched once rather than each time it appears.

**Sharing and saving.** Tapping Share hands a picture to your device's own share sheet; you choose who receives it, and it never passes through us. Saving a photograph uses add-only permission: the right to write one image to your library, not to read what is already there.

**Usage statistics.** To learn what people enjoy and where the game gets in their way, Time Rolls sends anonymous usage statistics to Google Firebase Analytics, a service run by Google. They cover: when the app is opened and how long it is used; which screens are opened (Home, Play, Themes, Progress, Setup); which categories are switched on or off; how each round goes — its category, the kind of question, whether it was right first time, how many tries and how many seconds it took, and whether one of your own photographs was in it (yes or no only); and when the day's challenge is finished.

Firebase also records the app version, the device model and iOS version, and a rough country or region worked out from the connection. To count visits without knowing who you are, it gives the app a random identifier. That identifier is not your name, your email, your Apple ID or the advertising identifier, and it changes if the app is deleted and installed again.

The statistics never include a photograph or anything about one: not which photo, person or place was shown, not a date, location, album name or caption, and not what the app recognised in a picture. They are not linked to you, not used for advertising, and not combined with anything from other apps or websites.

**Turning usage statistics off.** Open Setup → Privacy and switch off *Share usage statistics*. When it is off, nothing more is sent.

**Your own record of play.** The app also keeps your stars, streak and progress in a file on your device. That record stays on the device and is never sent anywhere. Deleting the app deletes it.

## 4. Legal bases (EU/UK/LGPD users)

Where the GDPR, UK GDPR or Brazil's LGPD applies, we rely on: **performance of a service** you request under our Terms of Use (operating the app); our **legitimate interests** in keeping the service working and secure; and our **legitimate interest** in understanding how the game is used so that we can improve it, for the anonymous usage statistics described above, which you can turn off at any time in Setup. Where we ever rely on **consent** — photo library access, the microphone, and “Places near you” are each asked for separately and are each optional — you may withdraw it at any time in iOS Settings.

## 5. How we store and protect data

Everything personal to you stays on your device, protected by your device's own encryption. We operate no account system and hold no database of users.

The photographs in our photo packs are stored with OneBucket, our storage provider and processor under a data processing agreement. That storage holds only freely licensed photographs that are already public; it holds nothing about you. Requests for them are encrypted in transit.

Usage statistics are processed by Google LLC as our service provider through Firebase Analytics, under Google's data processing terms, and are encrypted in transit. We have turned off Google's options to use this data for its own purposes or to share it.

We do not sell or share your personal information. We use no third-party advertising, and Firebase Analytics acts only as our service provider: the statistics are not used for advertising, cross-context behavioural advertising or any purpose of Google's own, so they do not constitute a “sale” or “share” under US state laws.

## 6. International transfers

Our photo-pack storage is processed in the United States and holds nothing about you. Usage statistics are processed by Google, which may store them in the United States or other countries; Google relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses for these transfers.

## 7. How long we keep data, and what deletion does

We hold almost nothing. The one exception is usage statistics: Google Analytics keeps them for 2 months and then deletes them. Because they are not linked to your name or to any account, we cannot look them up by person; switching off *Share usage statistics* stops any more from being sent.

Everything else the app knows about you is on your device, and deleting the app deletes all of it: your settings, your stars and streak, the caregiver's hidden-photo list, your own record of play, and the cached photo-pack images.

Photographs you shared or saved remain wherever you sent or saved them, as you would expect.

## 8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have rights to access, correct, delete and export your personal data, to object to or restrict certain processing, and to be free from unlawful discrimination for exercising these rights.

The only data that leaves your device about how you play is the anonymous usage statistics above. Because we cannot tell which statistics are yours, we cannot single them out to show you or delete them; they expire on their own after the retention period, and you can stop them at any time with *Share usage statistics* in Setup. Everything else you exercise directly on your device: photo, microphone and location permissions are all revocable in iOS Settings, and deleting the app erases everything it holds. For questions, contact [information@mission-peak.com](mailto:information@mission-peak.com). EU/UK users may also complain to their data protection authority. California residents may use an authorized agent and are entitled to non-discrimination for exercising their rights.

## 9. Children

Time Rolls is made for older adults and the people who care for them, and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children, and nothing the app collects — including its anonymous usage statistics — could identify a person of any age.

## 10. Accessibility and care settings

Some of what the app stores may be chosen by a family member or caregiver on somebody else's behalf: which photographs are never shown, how large the text is, how long the daily challenge is. This is kept on the device and is never sent anywhere. These choices are made in Setup, which anyone using the device can open.

## 11. Photo credits and licensing

The photographs in our packs come from Wikimedia Commons and are used under their licences. Those requiring attribution name their photographer in the app under Privacy → Photo credits; the remainder are public domain or CC0 and require none. No photograph of yours is ever included in a pack or shown to anybody else.
